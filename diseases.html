Skin Diseases Information

Information about the six skin conditions included in our dataset.

ACK - Actinic Keratosis

Actinic Keratosis is a rough, scaly skin patch that develops due to long-term exposure to sunlight.

Common characteristics: Dry, rough and scaly patches, often on sun-exposed skin.

Precautions: Use sunscreen, avoid excessive sun exposure and consult a dermatologist.

BCC - Basal Cell Carcinoma

Basal Cell Carcinoma is a type of skin cancer that commonly develops in areas exposed to sunlight.

Common characteristics: A shiny bump, a sore that does not heal or a reddish patch.

Precautions: Protect skin from sunlight and get unusual skin growths examined.

MEL - Melanoma

Melanoma is a serious type of skin cancer that develops in pigment-producing cells.

Common characteristics: A changing mole, irregular borders or uneven colour.

Precautions: Monitor changes in moles and seek medical advice for suspicious spots.

NEV - Melanocytic Nevus

A melanocytic nevus is commonly known as a mole. It is usually a harmless collection of pigment-producing cells.

Common characteristics: Brown or black spots that may be flat or raised.

Precautions: Monitor changes in size, shape or colour and consult a dermatologist if concerned.

SCC - Squamous Cell Carcinoma

Squamous Cell Carcinoma is a type of skin cancer that develops in the squamous cells of the skin.

Common characteristics: Scaly red patches, rough growths or sores that do not heal.

Precautions: Use sun protection and get persistent skin changes examined.

SEK - Seborrheic Keratosis

Seborrheic Keratosis is a common non-cancerous skin growth that often appears with age.

Common characteristics: Raised, waxy or rough growths that may be brown or black.

Precautions: Avoid scratching or removing growths yourself and seek medical advice if they change.
